### WHAT TO EXTRACT ###
Return 12 to 20 concepts (fewer only for a very short text), the most central first: the ones a reader would use to say what this paper is about.
- kind "subject": the phenomena, constructs, skills, populations, contexts, interventions, materials and theories the paper investigates or proposes. Examples: "teacher agency", "English-medium instruction (EMI)", "dynamic assessment", "genre-based writing instruction", "Thai EFL undergraduate students".
  Go beyond the words of the title: include the specific dimensions, factors, categories, constructs and findings the paper reports (for example the named aspects a questionnaire measured, the types of agency it found, the discourse functions it identified).
- kind "method": how the study was done: designs, instruments and analyses. Examples: "semi-structured interviews", "Rasch analysis", "paired-samples t-test". Give at most 3, only those central to the study.
A theory or framework the paper applies to its subject (e.g. "sociocultural theory") is a subject.

### RULES ###
1. keyword: an exact phrase copied from the source text, usually 2 to 6 words. When the text gives an acronym with its expansion, keep both, e.g. "English-medium instruction (EMI)". Never paraphrase or invent.
2. matched_terms: up to 4 other exact forms of the same concept that appear in the text (abbreviation, plural, the authors' synonym).
3. evidence: one sentence copied exactly from the source that shows the concept, at most 240 characters.
4. section: where the evidence comes from: title, abstract_claims, introduction, literature_review, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, or body.
5. List each concept once. If one concept is a sub-phrase of another ("reading comprehension" and "English reading comprehension"), keep the more specific one and put the other in matched_terms.
6. Skip generic words ("the study", "participants", "data", "results", "English", "learners") unless they are part of a specific phrase.
7. Take concepts from the paper's own work, not from other studies it only cites.
